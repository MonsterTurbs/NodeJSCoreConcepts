🌐IP vs. IP Address

  • IP = Internet Protocol (the set of rules in “Layer 3” that moves data packets).
  • IP Address = the unique numeric label (like your home address) assigned to each device.
  • Informally people say “IP” for shorthand, but technically they’re different concepts.

💻IPv4 Structure

  • Written in 32 bits, split into 4 “octets” (8 bits each): xxxxxxxx.xxxxxxxx.xxxxxxxx.xxxxxxxx.
  • Displayed in decimal as four numbers 0–255, separated by dots, e.g. 192.168.0.1.
  • Total addresses = 2³² ≈ 4.29 billion—now nearly exhausted, hence IPv6.

📏Subnet Masks & CIDR

  • A subnet mask marks which bits are “network” vs. “host.”
  • Examples:
    • /24 mask = 255.255.255.0 → first 24 bits network, last 8 bits hosts.
    • /16 mask = 255.255.0.0 → first 16 bits network, last 16 bits hosts.
  • Router uses the network portion to decide where to forward packets.

🔒Public vs. Private & NAT

  • Private ranges (not routable on the public Internet):
    • 10.0.0.0 – 10.255.255.255 ( /8 )
    • 172.16.0.0 – 172.31.255.255 ( /12 )
    • 192.168.0.0 – 192.168.255.255 ( /16 )
  • Devices on a home/office LAN use private IPs. A router’s NAT translates them to one public IP.
  • All devices behind that router share its public address to the Internet.

🔄Loopback & Special Addresses

  • 127.0.0.1 = Loopback (always refers back to your own device).
  • Used for testing local network code—never appears on the outside Internet.
  • IPv6 loopback = ::1 (128 bits).

🗺️IP Geolocation & VPNs

  • Public IPs often map to regions or ISPs. Websites can guess your country/city from your IP.
  • VPNs mask your true public IP by tunneling through another server—appearing from that server’s location.
  • VPNs operate at Layer 3 (IP); Node.js (Layer 4+) is not suited to build a VPN itself.

🚀Why IPv6?

  • IPv4’s 4 billion addresses are depleted by billions of devices & private LANs.
  • IPv6 uses 128 bits → 2¹²⁸ addresses (vastly more), and fixes IPv4 limitations.
  • Basics (structure, subnets) are similar—just far more address space.
