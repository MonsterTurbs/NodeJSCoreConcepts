🔓 How Hard is it to Break AES?

🕵️‍♂️Attacker’s Challenge

Without the secret key, AES ciphertext looks like random gibberish. To guess a missing 4 bits, try 2⁴=16 keys.

⌛Brute Force Basics

  • Try all key combos until plaintext makes sense (detect HTTP headers, meaningful data).
  • 16 tries took ~23 ms on a laptop in demo.

💻Real-World AES-128

  • Requires 2¹²⁸ attempts to guarantee finding the key.
  • Even on the world’s fastest supercomputer (El Capitan), two²¹⁶⁻¹ operations ≈ 30 trillion years.

🔢Why So Long?

  • 2^128 ≈ 3.4×10³⁸ possible keys.
  • Each AES encrypt ~1,000 integer steps; supercomputer does ~10¹⁷ ops/sec.
  • Even halving searches (lucky 50%) still impossibly long.

⚛️Quantum Threat?

Grover’s algorithm on a future quantum computer reduces to √2¹²⁸=2⁶⁴ ~1.8×10¹⁹ tries. Still trillions of years on best hardware.

🔐Key Security is Crucial

  • AES is mathematically strong—only practical attacks target the key (leaks, bad storage, lost secrets).
  • Keep keys truly random, private, and safe; never share or expose them (GitHub, logs, misconfig).

💡Takeaways

  • AES-128 is safe for decades; AES-256 adds even more breathing room.
  • Perfect encryption (OTP) is unbreakable, but AES is practically unbreakable if keys stay secret.
