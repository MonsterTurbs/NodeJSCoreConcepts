⚙️ Atomic Operations: Beginner-Friendly Notes

🔒 What Are Atomics?

  • ⚛️“Atomic” means indivisible—once started, cannot be interrupted.
  • ✅Either completes fully or not at all.
  • 💻Provided by CPU & OS, wrapped by JavaScript’s Atomics object.

🧱 Using Atomics in JS

  • 📦Work on shared typed arrays (e.g. Int32Array on a SharedArrayBuffer).
  • ⚙️Example: Atomics.add(myArray, 0, 1) adds 1 to element 0 atomically.
  • 🔗Accessible in browser & Node.js without extra libraries.

🛠️ Common Atomic Methods

  • ➕Atomics.add(typed, idx, val)
  • 💾Atomics.store(typed, idx, val)
  • 📥Atomics.load(typed, idx)
  • ➖Atomics.sub(typed, idx, val)
  • 🔄Atomics.compareExchange(typed, idx, old, new)

⏸️ Wait & Notify

  • 😴Atomics.wait(typed, idx, value) — thread sleeps until typed[idx] !== value.
  • 🔔Atomics.notify(typed, idx, count) — wakes up up to count sleepers.
  • 🔄Use wait/notify to implement mutexes for critical sections.

🚀 Why Use Atomics?

  • ⚡Eliminate race conditions in shared-memory code.
  • 🔐Ensure mutual exclusion—only one thread modifies critical data at once.
  • 📚Built-in in modern JS (ES2017+), portable across browsers & Node.js.
